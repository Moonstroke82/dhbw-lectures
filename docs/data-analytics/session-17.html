What a Strong Case Study Presentation Shows

Big idea: the question, the decision it serves and the answer — in one sentence, at the start
Data: source, licence, size, time span — and the limits that matter
Method: why this method fits the question, compared with a baseline
Evidence: honest evaluation on unseen data, with uncertainty
Scale or portal: why this tool — Spark or DuckDB with a measurement, or a dashboard demo
Recommendation: what to do, how sure we are, what comes next

Action titles: if the audience reads only your slide titles, do they get the story?

Own compilation based on Knaflic (2015), Chapman et al. (2000) and Provost and Fawcett (2013).

Five Questions That Ran Through Every Session

Decision: what will someone do differently because of this result?
Definitions: what exactly is measured — grain, formula, filters, time reference?
Time: was everything used known at the moment of the decision?
Baseline: how much better than the simple alternative, on unseen data?
Uncertainty: how sure are we — interval, coverage, trust checks — and is the method suitable?

These five questions are also the best checklist for your semester-6 project — and for any analysis you will be asked to trust.

Own synthesis based on Chapman et al. (2000), Kimball and Ross (2013), Kohavi et al. (2020) and Hyndman and Athanasopoulos (2021).

Exercise: One Case, Four Parts

EXERCISE

A city's bike-sharing operator has trip data for five years (60 million trips) and wants to put the right number of bikes at each station every morning. In pairs:

Part A: which fact table and grain, and which KPI would you put on the operations dashboard?
Part B: which station-level prediction or classification could help — and which leaky feature would you exclude?
Part C: what would you forecast, at what granularity and horizon, against which baseline?
Part B again: how would you test whether a new rebalancing rule reduces empty stations?
Part D: DuckDB or Spark — what decides?

Key Takeaways

•A case study convinces with a big idea, an honest evaluation against a baseline and a clear recommendation with its limits
•Good feedback is specific and actionable — on the work, not the person
•The course follows one thread: data → method → evaluation → decision, from warehouse to forecast to scale
•Five questions travel with you: decision, definitions, time, baseline, uncertainty
•Semester 6 turns this into a real project: plan it, review it with stakeholders, and keep partner information confidential
