What Is an IT Strategy?

Content and derivation

IT Strategy: Definition and Content

An IT strategy describes the long-term direction of a company's IT: which IT is needed to support and enable the business strategy, and how it is provided.

Content areaExample questions
IT goalsWhich business goals does IT support? Which KPIs show it?
Applications and dataWhich application portfolio and data platforms do we need?
Technology and architectureWhich standards, platforms, cloud strategy?
SourcingWhat do we do ourselves, what do we buy or outsource?
Organisation and skillsHow is IT organised, which skills do we need?
Investments and roadmapWhich projects, in which order, with which budget?

Own compilation based on Krcmar (2015) and Tiemeyer (2023).

Business–IT Alignment

The strategic alignment model

What Is Business–IT Alignment?

Strategic IT alignment is "the degree to which the business strategy and plans, and the IT strategy and plans, complement each other."

•Alignment is not a state reached once, but a continuous process
•Studies associate higher alignment with better firm performance
•Alignment has a strategic (plans, goals) and a social side (shared understanding between business and IT people)

Chan and Reich (2007, p. 300).

Critical View on Alignment

OPTIONAL · SELF-STUDY
•Rigidity: tight alignment to today's strategy can make IT inflexible when the strategy changes
•Informal alignment — shared understanding and relationships — often matters more than formal plans
•In digital business, business and IT strategy increasingly merge into one digital business strategy

Chan and Reich (2007); Urbach and Ahlemann (2019).

Measuring Alignment Maturity

5 · Optimised process
Alignment is continuous and adaptive; IT and business co-create strategy
4 · Improved / managed process
IT is a value centre; governance and value measurement work
3 · Established, focused process
Alignment processes exist; IT is seen as an asset
2 · Committed process
First commitment to alignment; awareness on both sides
1 · Initial / ad hoc process
Business and IT are not aligned

Assessed along six criteria: communications, value measurement, governance, partnership, scope and architecture, skills.

Based on Luftman (2000).

Case Exercise: Alignment Analysis

EXERCISE

Case: Kurpfalz Sanitär AG is a wholesaler for bathroom fittings. Its new business strategy: become the leading online platform for installers by 2030, with same-day delivery.

•IT runs a 20-year-old ERP system, heavily customised; the web shop is a separate system
•The IT budget is set as 1.5 % of revenue each year; projects are approved by the CFO
•IT and sales hardly talk; the sales director calls IT "the department of no"

Tasks:

Which alignment perspective fits the new strategy? Justify.
Where do you see gaps in strategic fit and functional integration?
Estimate the alignment maturity level and name two criteria that pull it down.
Suggest three measures to improve alignment.

Key Takeaways

•An IT strategy sets the long-term direction of IT: goals, applications, architecture, sourcing, organisation, investments
•It is derived from the business strategy — but IT can also enable new business strategies
•The strategic alignment model requires strategic fit and functional integration across four domains
•Alignment maturity can be assessed along six criteria and five levels
