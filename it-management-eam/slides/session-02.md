# What Is an IT Strategy? {.section}

Content and derivation

---

# IT Strategy: Definition and Content

::: callout
An IT strategy describes the **long-term direction** of a company's IT: which IT is needed to support and enable the business strategy, and how it is provided.
:::

| Content area | Example questions |
|---|---|
| IT goals | Which business goals does IT support? Which KPIs show it? |
| Applications and data | Which application portfolio and data platforms do we need? |
| Technology and architecture | Which standards, platforms, cloud strategy? |
| Sourcing | What do we do ourselves, what do we buy or outsource? |
| Organisation and skills | How is IT organised, which skills do we need? |
| Investments and roadmap | Which projects, in which order, with which budget? |

::: source
Own compilation based on Krcmar (2015) and Tiemeyer (2023).
:::

::: notes
Point out that the content areas map to later sessions: governance and organisation (3), controlling and portfolio (4), architecture and landscape (10–14), sourcing (15).
:::

# Deriving the IT Strategy

::: layers
- 4 · Implement and control: Roadmap, projects, IT portfolio, KPIs, regular review
- 3 · Formulate IT strategy: IT goals, principles, target picture, sourcing and investment decisions
- 2 · Analyse the IT situation: As-is applications, infrastructure, costs, skills, risks; technology trends
- 1 · Understand the business strategy: Goals, business model, competitive position, planned changes
:::

::: source
Own illustration based on Krcmar (2015) and Tiemeyer (2023).
:::

::: notes
Read bottom-up. In reality the process is iterative, not linear: IT can also **enable** new business strategies (e.g. a data-based service), which is the "inside-out" view. This prepares the strategic alignment model, where the direction can go both ways.
:::

# Business–IT Alignment {.section}

The strategic alignment model

---

# What Is Business–IT Alignment?

::: callout
Strategic IT alignment is "the degree to which the business strategy and plans, and the IT strategy and plans, complement each other."
:::

- Alignment is not a state reached once, but a **continuous process**
- Studies associate higher alignment with better firm performance
- Alignment has a **strategic** (plans, goals) and a **social** side (shared understanding between business and IT people)

::: source
Chan and Reich (2007, p. 300).
:::

::: notes
Chan and Reich review two decades of alignment research. The social dimension — whether business and IT executives understand and trust each other — turned out to be as important as formal planning.
:::

---

# The Strategic Alignment Model

| | **Business** | **IT** |
|---|---|---|
| **External (strategy)** | Business strategy: scope, distinctive competences, governance | IT strategy: technology scope, systemic competences, IT governance |
| **Internal (infrastructure and processes)** | Organisational infrastructure and processes | IS infrastructure and processes |

- **Strategic fit:** external strategy and internal infrastructure must fit (vertical)
- **Functional integration:** business and IT must fit (horizontal)

::: source
Adapted from Henderson and Venkatraman (1993).
:::

::: notes
Henderson and Venkatraman's model has four domains. Their key idea: alignment needs both strategic fit and functional integration. A firm cannot look at only one domain pair, e.g. just aligning IT infrastructure to business processes, without considering strategy.
:::

---

# Four Alignment Perspectives

| Perspective | Driver | Logic |
|---|---|---|
| Strategy execution | Business strategy | Business strategy → organisational infrastructure → IS infrastructure |
| Technology transformation | Business strategy | Business strategy → IT strategy → IS infrastructure |
| Competitive potential | IT strategy | IT strategy → business strategy → organisational infrastructure |
| Service level | IT strategy | IT strategy → IS infrastructure → organisational infrastructure |

::: source
Based on Henderson and Venkatraman (1993).
:::

::: notes
The first two perspectives are "business-led" (IT follows the business), the last two "IT-led" (IT enables new business strategies). Examples: strategy execution — classic ERP rollout to support a new process; competitive potential — a manufacturer uses IoT data to offer "machine as a service". Ask students which perspective dominates in their companies.
:::

---

# Critical View on Alignment {.optional}

- **Rigidity:** tight alignment to today's strategy can make IT inflexible when the strategy changes
- **Informal alignment** — shared understanding and relationships — often matters more than formal plans
- In digital business, business and IT strategy increasingly **merge** into one digital business strategy

::: source
Chan and Reich (2007); Urbach and Ahlemann (2019).
:::

::: notes
Self-study. Chan and Reich discuss these points as open issues of alignment research. The merging of business and IT strategy is a recurring theme in the EAM part (session 12).
:::

# Measuring Alignment Maturity

::: layers
- 5 · Optimised process: Alignment is continuous and adaptive; IT and business co-create strategy
- 4 · Improved / managed process: IT is a value centre; governance and value measurement work
- 3 · Established, focused process: Alignment processes exist; IT is seen as an asset
- 2 · Committed process: First commitment to alignment; awareness on both sides
- 1 · Initial / ad hoc process: Business and IT are not aligned
:::

Assessed along six criteria: **communications, value measurement, governance, partnership, scope and architecture, skills**.

::: source
Based on Luftman (2000).
:::

::: notes
Luftman's strategic alignment maturity model works like a maturity model in software engineering: an organisation is assessed on six criteria, and the overall level shows where to improve. Exam tip: students should know the idea of the levels and be able to name some of the criteria, not reproduce every detail.
:::

# Case Exercise: Alignment Analysis {.exercise}

**Case:** *Kurpfalz Sanitär AG* is a wholesaler for bathroom fittings. Its new business strategy: become the leading **online platform** for installers by 2030, with same-day delivery.

- IT runs a 20-year-old ERP system, heavily customised; the web shop is a separate system
- The IT budget is set as 1.5 % of revenue each year; projects are approved by the CFO
- IT and sales hardly talk; the sales director calls IT "the department of no"

**Tasks:**
1. Which **alignment perspective** fits the new strategy? Justify.
2. Where do you see gaps in **strategic fit** and **functional integration**?
3. Estimate the **alignment maturity level** and name two criteria that pull it down.
4. Suggest three measures to improve alignment.

::: notes
Groups of 4–5, about 30 min + 10 min discussion.
Expected answers (several defensible): 1 technology transformation (business strategy requires a new IT strategy: platform, integration) or competitive potential if IT capabilities shape the platform idea. 2 Strategic fit: legacy ERP and separate shop do not support same-day delivery and platform; functional integration: no joint planning between business and IT. 3 Level 1–2; weak criteria: communications, partnership, value measurement (budget as fixed percentage), governance (CFO-only approval). 4 Joint business–IT steering committee, IT strategy derived from platform strategy with roadmap, value-based IT portfolio management, business relationship managers.
:::

# Key Takeaways

- An **IT strategy** sets the long-term direction of IT: goals, applications, architecture, sourcing, organisation, investments
- It is derived from the business strategy — but IT can also **enable** new business strategies
- The **strategic alignment model** requires strategic fit and functional integration across four domains
- **Alignment maturity** can be assessed along six criteria and five levels
