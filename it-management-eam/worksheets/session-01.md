This worksheet lets you practise the concepts of session 1: the debate about the role of IT, the strategic impact grid, Krcmar's model of information management, the fields of action of IT management, and the difference between strategic and operational IT management. Work on your own or in a small group. The last task is in the style of an exam question; try it without your notes first.

## Levels of Information Management

Assign each activity to one level of Krcmar's (2015) model of information management — **information economy**, **information systems** or **ICT** — or to the **cross-level management tasks**. Give a short reason.

- a) Deciding which sales figures regional managers need each Monday
- b) Replacing the network switches in the head office
- c) Planning the life cycle of the CRM application until its retirement
- d) Setting up an IT steering committee
- e) Checking which management reports are never opened
- f) Choosing a storage platform for the data centre
- g) Defining a common data model for customer master data
- h) Planning how many SAP specialists IT needs in three years

::: answer lines=8
:::

::: hint
Ask: is it about **what information** the business needs (demand, supply, use), about **applications and data**, about **technical infrastructure**, or about **how IT as a whole is managed**?
:::

::: solution
- a) **Information economy**: information demand of management.
- b) **ICT**: network infrastructure.
- c) **Information systems**: managing an application over its life cycle.
- d) **Management tasks** (IT governance): decision structures across all levels.
- e) **Information economy**: information use; reports nobody reads are a sign of a mismatch between supply and demand.
- f) **ICT**: storage technology.
- g) **Information systems**: data management is part of the information systems level.
- h) **Management tasks** (IT personnel).

Accept other assignments only with a convincing reason, e.g. h) can be linked to information systems if the student argues about the SAP system itself; the stronger answer is IT personnel as a cross-level task.
:::

## Strategic or Operational?

Decide for each statement whether it describes **strategic** or **operational** IT management. Then mark whether it is mainly a **run** or a **change** task.

1. The service desk is asked to cut the average waiting time on the hotline to two minutes.
2. The board decides to move all applications to the public cloud within five years.
3. An expired SSL certificate on the web shop is renewed.
4. IT defines a target architecture with one ERP system for all subsidiaries.
5. The monthly IT budget report shows a 5 % overrun on licences; the IT controller investigates.
6. The company decides to build its own data science team instead of buying analytics services.

::: answer lines=6
:::

::: solution
1. Operational, run (service quality in daily operations).
2. Strategic, change (long-term direction, sourcing).
3. Operational, run.
4. Strategic, change (target architecture, several years).
5. Operational, run (budget control within the year). A student who argues that repeated overruns trigger a strategic licence decision shows good understanding.
6. Strategic, change (sourcing and skills decision).

Marking: one point per correct strategic/operational decision; the run/change classification is a bonus check of understanding.
:::

## The Strategic Impact Grid

Place each company in the strategic impact grid of Nolan and McFarlan (2005) (support, factory, turnaround, strategic) and justify your choice in one or two sentences.

- a) A **hospital** with electronic patient records, networked medical devices and a planned telemedicine service
- b) A regional **bakery chain** with 40 shops, cash registers and an accounting package
- c) An online-only **bank** without branches
- d) A **car parts supplier** delivering just in time to car plants; no new IT projects are planned
- e) A **law firm** that wants to offer AI-based contract analysis as a new product

::: answer lines=8
:::

::: hint
Two questions: how badly would the business suffer if today's IT failed for a day (need for reliable IT)? How much does the future business depend on new IT (need for new IT)?
:::

::: solution
- a) **Strategic**: an outage of patient records and devices endangers operations and patients; telemedicine requires new IT.
- b) **Support**: the shops can sell for a day without much IT; few new IT initiatives. If the chain started online pre-ordering, it would move towards turnaround.
- c) **Strategic**: the bank *is* its IT; every new product is an IT product.
- d) **Factory**: just-in-time delivery stops if IT fails, but there is little need for new IT.
- e) **Turnaround**: today's legal work depends little on IT, but the new product does.

Marking: the quadrant is less important than a justification that uses both dimensions of the grid.
:::

## Does IT Matter?

A mid-sized **insurance company** discusses its IT budget. The CFO quotes Carr (2003): "IT is a commodity; we should spend less and follow others." The head of claims says that a fast online claims process is the main reason customers stay.

1. Summarise Carr's argument in two sentences.
2. Give two arguments **for** and two **against** the CFO's view in this specific company.
3. Write a short recommendation: where should the insurer follow Carr, and where not?

::: answer lines=10
:::

::: solution
1. Carr argues that IT has become a widely available commodity, like electricity; because every company can buy the same technology, it no longer creates competitive advantage. Companies should therefore spend less, follow rather than lead and focus on managing IT risks.
2. For the CFO: infrastructure (data centre, office software, e-mail) is standard and can be bought cheaply, e.g. from the cloud; being a technology pioneer is risky and expensive. Against: the online claims process differentiates the insurer from competitors; advantage comes from how IT is used in processes and products, not from the technology itself; data on claims can be used for pricing and fraud detection.
3. Recommendation: treat infrastructure and standard applications as a commodity (buy, standardise, minimise cost); invest selectively in IT that supports differentiating processes such as claims handling and customer experience. Good answers name this split explicitly.
:::

## Mapping Problems to Fields of Action

The new managing director of **Stadtwerke Neckartal**, a municipal utility (electricity, water, public swimming pools), lists the IT-related complaints she heard in her first month. Assign each complaint to the **field of action** of IT management (Krcmar, 2015; Tiemeyer, 2023) that should deal with it, and say whether a **strategic** or an **operational** response is needed first.

1. "Nobody can tell me what IT costs us per customer."
2. "Our smart meter project has no clear goal — is it about saving costs or new tariffs?"
3. "The ticket machines at the pools fail every weekend."
4. "The regulator asks for proof that our control systems are protected against cyber attacks."
5. "Every department buys its own software; IT learns about it when it breaks."
6. "There are 140 applications, and no one knows which of them still talk to each other."
7. "Our customer portal looks different from the billing letters, and the data does not match."
8. "We have no plan for what happens if the data centre floods."

::: answer lines=10
:::

::: solution
1. **IT controlling** (cost transparency, service view of costs); operational first, later a strategic chargeback decision.
2. **IT strategy and alignment**; strategic.
3. **IT service management**; operational (incidents, problem analysis).
4. **IT risk and compliance** and **IT security**; first operational (evidence for the regulator), then strategic (security management system).
5. **IT governance and organisation** (decision rights for software purchases); strategic.
6. **Enterprise architecture management** (landscape documentation, interfaces); first operational (inventory), then strategic (target landscape).
7. Several fields are defensible: **EAM** (integration of portal and billing data) or **IT quality management** (data and service quality). Accept either with a reason.
8. **IT security and business continuity**; strategic (business continuity planning) with operational measures.
:::

## Exam-Style Task: Weinbau Kaiserstuhl eG

*Weinbau Kaiserstuhl eG* is a winegrowers' cooperative with 300 members and 60 employees. It runs a winery, a shop and a small online shop. IT consists of an ERP package for orders and accounting, a web shop run by an agency, and sensors in the cellar that monitor fermentation temperatures. One IT employee and an external IT service company look after the systems. The board wants to sell 30 % of its wine online within five years and to offer members a portal with their delivery and quality data.

1. Explain the three levels of Krcmar's model of information management and give one example from the case for each level. (3 points)
2. Place the cooperative in the strategic impact grid **today** and **in five years**. Justify both positions. (3 points)
3. Distinguish strategic and operational IT management and give **two** examples of each from the case. (4 points)

::: answer lines=16
:::

::: solution
Marking guide (10 points):

**1. Krcmar's levels (3 points, 1 per level with correct example)**

- Information economy: which information is needed, offered and used, e.g. members want delivery and quality data; the board needs online sales figures.
- Information systems: applications and data, e.g. ERP, web shop, planned member portal.
- ICT: infrastructure, e.g. cellar sensors and their network, servers or cloud hosting.
- Half a point if the level is named correctly but the example is missing or wrong.

**2. Strategic impact grid (3 points)**

- Today: **support** or **factory** (1 point). Factory is defensible if the student argues that failure of the temperature sensors endangers the wine; support if the focus is on administration.
- In five years: **strategic** (1 point), because online sales and the member portal require new IT and the business depends on reliable IT.
- Justification uses both dimensions (need for reliable IT, need for new IT): 1 point.

**3. Strategic vs. operational (4 points)**

- Correct distinction (long-term direction, "doing the right things", top management vs. short- to mid-term delivery, "doing things right", IT staff and service providers): 1 point.
- Two strategic examples, e.g. decision to grow online sales to 30 %, building a member portal, whether to keep the agency or bring the shop in-house: 1.5 points.
- Two operational examples, e.g. keeping the web shop running, maintaining the sensors, processing member data updates, managing the contract with the IT service company day to day: 1.5 points.
:::
