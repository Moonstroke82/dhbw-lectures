This worksheet lets you practise the concepts of session 2: the content and derivation of an IT strategy, the strategic alignment model of Henderson and Venkatraman (1993) with its four alignment perspectives, and Luftman's (2000) alignment maturity model. The last task is in the style of an exam question.

## What Belongs in an IT Strategy?

The following sentences come from the IT strategy of a mail-order pharmacy. Assign each to a **content area** of an IT strategy (Krcmar, 2015; Tiemeyer, 2023): IT goals · applications and data · technology and architecture · sourcing · organisation and skills · investments and roadmap.

1. "By 2028, 90 % of prescriptions are processed without manual data entry."
2. "All new applications run on our cloud platform; on-premises servers are phased out."
3. "Operation of the warehouse system is outsourced; the order platform stays in-house."
4. "We build a product team of developers, pharmacists and data analysts for the app."
5. "A central customer data platform replaces the three customer databases."
6. "Phase 1 (2026): e-prescription interface, 1.2 m€; phase 2 (2027): app relaunch, 2.0 m€."

::: answer lines=6
:::

::: solution
1. IT goals (a measurable goal that supports a business goal).
2. Technology and architecture (platform standard).
3. Sourcing.
4. Organisation and skills.
5. Applications and data.
6. Investments and roadmap.

Some sentences touch two areas (e.g. 5 also implies architecture); accept a second area if the first is right.
:::

## Deriving an IT Strategy

A furniture manufacturer starts its first IT strategy process. Assign each activity to one of the four steps used in the session: **(1) understand the business strategy · (2) analyse the IT situation · (3) formulate the IT strategy · (4) implement and control**.

- a) Interview the sales director about the planned direct-to-consumer online business
- b) List all applications with their age, costs and known problems
- c) Agree the principle "standard software before own development"
- d) Track the share of IT projects delivered on time each quarter
- e) Check which technology trends (e.g. 3D configurators) competitors use
- f) Decide to replace the self-built order system by 2028
- g) Set up a roadmap with three project waves and budgets

Then explain in two or three sentences why the process is not strictly top-down in practice.

::: answer lines=8
:::

::: solution
- a) 1 · b) 2 · c) 3 · d) 4 · e) 2 (analysis of the IT situation includes technology trends) · f) 3 · g) 4 (the roadmap turns the strategy into projects; 3 is also accepted if the student sees the roadmap as part of formulating the strategy)

Not strictly top-down: the analysis of IT (step 2) can show opportunities that change the business strategy, e.g. a 3D configurator enables a new online sales model. IT can **enable** new business strategies (inside-out view), which corresponds to the IT-led alignment perspectives. The process is iterative and revised regularly.
:::

## The Four Domains of the Strategic Alignment Model

Assign each item to one of the four domains of the strategic alignment model: **business strategy · IT strategy · organisational infrastructure and processes · IS infrastructure and processes**.

1. A logistics company decides to offer same-day delivery in all major cities.
2. The company standardises on one cloud provider and an API-first approach.
3. The dispatch process is reorganised into regional teams.
4. A route planning application and a data warehouse are introduced.
5. The company decides that IT investments above 500 k€ are approved jointly by the executive board and the CIO.
6. Warehouse staff are trained to work with handheld scanners.

Which pairs of domains are linked by **strategic fit**, and which by **functional integration**?

::: answer lines=8
:::

::: solution
1. Business strategy (scope: new service).
2. IT strategy (technology scope, systemic competences).
3. Organisational infrastructure and processes.
4. IS infrastructure and processes.
5. IT strategy (IT governance is part of the IT strategy domain in Henderson and Venkatraman's model). Business strategy is also accepted if the student argues with business governance.
6. Organisational infrastructure and processes (skills).

Strategic fit links the external (strategy) and internal (infrastructure) domain on the **same** side: business strategy ↔ organisational infrastructure, IT strategy ↔ IS infrastructure. Functional integration links business and IT on the **same** level: business strategy ↔ IT strategy, organisational infrastructure ↔ IS infrastructure.
:::

## Which Alignment Perspective?

Name the alignment perspective (strategy execution, technology transformation, competitive potential, service level) for each situation and justify it with the chain of domains.

- a) An insurer's new strategy is to cut costs. It redesigns its claims process into a central claims centre; IT then adapts the claims application to the new process.
- b) A fashion retailer decides on an omnichannel strategy. To support it, IT chooses a cloud commerce platform and an API architecture, which are then implemented.
- c) A machine manufacturer notices that its machines already send sensor data. It decides to sell "uptime guaranteed" contracts based on predictive maintenance and reorganises its service department.
- d) The IT department of a university sets itself the goal of "excellent self-service" and introduces a service portal. Faculties change their administrative procedures to use it.

::: answer lines=8
:::

::: solution
- a) **Strategy execution**: business strategy → organisational infrastructure (claims centre) → IS infrastructure (application adapted).
- b) **Technology transformation**: business strategy → IT strategy (platform and architecture choice) → IS infrastructure.
- c) **Competitive potential**: IT capabilities (sensor data, IT strategy) → new business strategy (uptime contracts) → organisational infrastructure (service department).
- d) **Service level**: IT strategy → IS infrastructure (portal) → organisational infrastructure (faculty procedures).

Marking: one point per perspective, half of it for the justification via the chain of domains.
:::

## Assessing Alignment Maturity

At *Odenwald Elektro GmbH*, a manufacturer of switchgear, you hear the following statements in interviews. For each statement, name the **criterion** of Luftman's (2000) alignment maturity model it relates to (communications, value measurement, governance, partnership, scope and architecture, skills). Then estimate the overall maturity level and justify it.

1. "IT reports server availability; nobody measures what IT contributes to the business."
2. "The CIO is invited to the strategy workshop once a year, but only for the last hour."
3. "IT projects are approved by the CFO alone, based on cost."
4. "We have five different CAD systems because each plant chose its own."
5. "Our IT staff know the technology well but hardly understand how switchgear is sold."
6. "Business and IT people meet only when something is broken."

::: answer lines=8
:::

::: solution
1. Value measurement
2. Partnership (IT is not a strategic partner); communications is also accepted
3. Governance
4. Scope and architecture
5. Skills
6. Communications

Overall: **level 1–2** (initial/ad hoc to committed). Justification: none of the six criteria shows an established alignment process; IT is seen as a cost centre, not as an asset or partner. Level 2 is defensible if the student argues that the CIO's invitation to the strategy workshop shows first commitment.
:::

## Exam-Style Task: Bodensee Reisen AG

*Bodensee Reisen AG* is a tour operator with 25 travel agencies and a call centre. Its new business strategy is to sell personalised trips mainly online and through an app by 2030; the agencies will become "experience stores". IT runs a booking system from 2005 that only travel agents can use. The IT budget is fixed at 2 % of revenue. The head of IT reports to the head of finance and is not involved in strategy meetings. The marketing department has started its own projects with an external agency for an app prototype.

1. Define business–IT alignment. (2 points)
2. Explain **strategic fit** and **functional integration** and give one gap in the case for each. (4 points)
3. Which **alignment perspective** should the company follow? Justify. (2 points)
4. Estimate the alignment **maturity level** and name two criteria that pull it down, with evidence from the case. (2 points)
5. Recommend **two measures** to improve alignment. (2 points)

::: answer lines=18
:::

::: solution
Marking guide (12 points):

**1. Definition (2 points)**

- The degree to which business strategy and plans and IT strategy and plans complement each other (Chan & Reich, 2007): 1 point.
- Alignment as a continuous process, with a strategic and a social side: 1 point.

**2. Strategic fit and functional integration (4 points)**

- Strategic fit = fit between external strategy and internal infrastructure and processes within business or within IT (1 point). Gap: the 2005 booking system (IS infrastructure) does not support an online and app strategy; or the agencies' processes do not yet fit the "experience store" strategy (1 point).
- Functional integration = fit between the business and the IT domain at the same level (1 point). Gap: the business strategy (online, personalised) and the IT strategy (fixed budget, no IT involvement in strategy) are not linked; marketing builds an app without IT (1 point).

**3. Perspective (2 points)**

- **Technology transformation** (business strategy → IT strategy → IS infrastructure): the online strategy needs a new IT strategy (platform, app, data for personalisation) before the infrastructure can be built (2 points).
- Competitive potential is accepted for 1 point if the student argues that data-based personalisation is an IT-enabled opportunity, but must explain why IT leads.

**4. Maturity (2 points)**

- Level 1–2 (1 point).
- Two criteria with evidence (0.5 each): governance (IT head reports to finance, budget as fixed percentage), partnership/communications (IT not in strategy meetings, marketing works around IT), value measurement (budget not linked to value), scope and architecture (old booking system for agents only).

**5. Measures (2 points, 1 each)**

- e.g. CIO or head of digital in the executive board or strategy meetings; joint business–IT steering committee for the online strategy; derive an IT strategy and roadmap from the business strategy; value-based instead of percentage budget; bring marketing's app project into a joint product team.
:::

## Optional Challenge: Can Alignment Go Too Far? {.optional}

Chan and Reich (2007) discuss that very tight alignment can be a problem. Explain the risk with an example, and describe how a company can stay aligned **and** flexible.

::: answer lines=6
:::

::: solution
Risk: if IT is optimised for today's strategy (e.g. a system tightly customised for one sales channel), it becomes rigid when the strategy changes; the investment then blocks the new strategy. Example: a retailer whose IT is perfectly aligned with store sales finds it hard to move online.

Staying flexible: modular architecture and standard interfaces, shared understanding between business and IT (informal alignment) so that changes are recognised early, regular strategy reviews instead of a once-only plan, and keeping some budget for experiments.
:::
