This worksheet lets you practise the concepts of session 12: business models and their building blocks (Teece, 2010; Osterwalder & Pigneur, 2010), the difference between digitisation, digitalisation and digital transformation (Vial, 2019), digital business models (Weill & Woerner, 2018), business capabilities and capability maps, heat maps, capability-based planning and value streams (Hanschke, 2023; The Open Group, 2022), and the role of EA in business–IT alignment (Henderson & Venkatraman, 1993; Bharadwaj et al., 2013). Terms follow the session slides: a capability says **what** an enterprise must be able to do; processes, organisation units and applications say **how**, **who** and **with which software**. Paper and pencil are enough; for the maps, sticky notes or a simple drawing tool help. The last core task is in the style of an exam question.

## True or False?

Decide whether each statement is **true** or **false**. Correct every false statement in one sentence.

- a) A business capability map should mirror the organisation chart, so that every department finds itself on it.
- b) A business model describes how an enterprise creates and delivers value to customers and how it captures a share of that value.
- c) Replacing paper delivery notes with scanned PDF files is an example of digital transformation.
- d) A value stream describes the stages through which a stakeholder receives value, and each stage is enabled by one or more capabilities.
- e) Business capabilities usually change more often than business processes.
- f) In a heat map, the same capability map is coloured according to one attribute, for example strategic importance or IT support.

::: answer lines=8
:::

::: hint
Apply the test from the session: would a reorganisation or a new system rename it? And ask whether the value proposition or only the process changes.
:::

::: solution
- a) **False.** A capability map describes **what** the enterprise must be able to do, independent of the organisation; organisation units are mapped to capabilities, but copying the org chart makes the map change with every reorganisation.
- b) **True.**
- c) **False.** It is **digitisation** (information from analogue to digital form). Digital transformation means that digital technology triggers significant changes, typically to the value proposition and the business model.
- d) **True.**
- e) **False.** Capabilities are **stable**; processes, organisation and applications that realise them change much more often (e.g. fare management has existed for decades, its processes and systems have changed many times).
- f) **True.**

Marking hint: one point per item; for false statements the point requires the correction.
:::

## How Far Does the Change Go? A Winegrowers' Cooperative

*Weinbau Kaiserstuhl eG* (sessions 1 and 11), a winegrowers' cooperative with 300 members and 60 employees, wants to sell 30 % of its wine online within five years. Today it sells mainly through its own shop at the winery, to restaurants and through wholesalers; the web shop is run by an agency. The board discusses four measures:

- M1: Members' grape delivery slips are scanned instead of being filed on paper.
- M2: The cellar sensors send an alert to the cellar master's phone when a fermentation tank gets too warm.
- M3: A **wine subscription**: customers receive six bottles chosen by the cellar master every quarter, pay automatically and rate the wines in an app.
- M4: A **regional wine platform** on which neighbouring winegrowers and local restaurants also offer wines and tasting events; the cooperative earns a commission.

1. Classify each measure as **digitisation**, **digitalisation** or **digital transformation**. Justify in one sentence each.
2. For M3 and M4, name the **building blocks** of the Business Model Canvas that change, with one concrete change each (at least three building blocks per measure).
3. *(Optional, uses the optional slide on digital business models.)* Using Weill and Woerner (2018): in which digital business model is the cooperative **today** with respect to its sales through wholesalers, and which model would M4 move it towards?

::: answer lines=14
:::

::: hint
The canvas has nine building blocks: customer segments, value propositions, channels, customer relationships, revenue streams, key resources, key activities, key partnerships, cost structure.
:::

::: solution
1. M1 **digitisation** — the same information in digital form; nothing else changes. M2 **digitalisation** — a process (monitoring fermentation) is improved with digital technology; the value proposition stays the same. M3 **digital transformation** — a new value proposition (curated subscription), a new revenue stream (recurring payments) and a direct customer relationship. M4 **digital transformation** — a new role as a platform with partners and commission income. Accept "M3 digitalisation of sales" only if the student argues that the subscription is a minor variant of the existing online shop; the recurring revenue and customer data make transformation the better answer.
2. M3: **value proposition** (selection by the cellar master, convenience); **customer relationships** (from one-off purchases to a subscription with ratings); **revenue streams** (recurring quarterly payments); **channels** (app); **key activities** (curating, shipping every quarter, managing subscriptions); **key resources** (customer data, app); **cost structure** (app, shipping, payment fees). M4: **key partnerships** (neighbouring winegrowers, restaurants); **revenue streams** (commission); **value proposition** (regional wine and events in one place); **customer segments** (also tourists and customers of other wineries; partners as a second customer group); **key activities** (operating the platform, onboarding partners); **key resources** (platform, brand).
3. Through wholesalers the cooperative knows little about its end customers and sells within its own value chain: **supplier**. M4 moves it towards **ecosystem driver** (deep customer knowledge and offers of many partners); M3 alone would move it towards **omnichannel**.

Marking hint: one point per measure in part 1 (with justification); three points per measure in part 2 (one per building block with a concrete change); two points for part 3.
:::

## Cleaning Up a Capability Map at Rheinland Logistik

*Rheinland Logistik GmbH* (sessions 1, 4 and 11), a logistics company with 1,800 employees, 25 warehouses and 300 trucks, has asked a working student to draft a level-1 capability map. The draft contains these boxes:

| # | Draft box | # | Draft box |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Warehouse management | 7 | IT department |
| 2 | WMS Lagerverwaltung | 8 | Route planning |
| 3 | Load trucks at the ramp before 6:00 | 9 | Fleet management |
| 4 | Customer management | 10 | Invoice customers in SAP |
| 5 | Sales department | 11 | Human resources management |
| 6 | Fleet telematics | 12 | Strategy |

1. For each box, decide whether it is a **business capability**. If not, say what it is (**organisation unit, process, application**) and propose a capability name instead — or say that it belongs to an existing capability.
2. Sort the resulting capabilities into **strategic, core and supporting** tiers. Add **two** capabilities that are clearly missing for a logistics company.
3. Write **two rules** for the working student that would have prevented the mistakes.

::: answer lines=16
:::

::: hint
A capability answers "what must we be able to do?". Look for software names, departments, and boxes that describe steps with times or systems.
:::

::: solution
1. 1 **capability** (warehouse management). 2 **application** (the WMS from task 4 of worksheet 11) → supports *warehouse management*; remove from the map. 3 **process step** (with a time and a place) → part of *warehouse management* or *transport execution*, e.g. level 2 "loading and dispatch". 4 **capability**. 5 **organisation unit** → capability *sales management* (or *customer acquisition*). 6 **application** → supports *fleet management*; remove. 7 **organisation unit** → capability *IT management* (or *IT and information management*). 8 **capability** (accept also as level 2 under *transport management*). 9 **capability**. 10 **process step + system** → capability *billing* or *financial management* (level 2: invoicing). 11 **capability**. 12 too vague; rename into a capability, e.g. *strategy and business development* (or *corporate strategy management*).
2. Example: **strategic** — strategy and business development; (risk and compliance management; network and site planning). **Core** — customer management; sales management; warehouse management; transport management with route planning; fleet management. **Supporting** — financial management (incl. billing); human resources management; IT management; procurement. Missing, e.g.: **transport execution / delivery** (the actual carrying of goods), **procurement**, **contract and pricing management** for logistics contracts, **customs and compliance**, **subcontractor (carrier) management**, **damage and claims handling**.
3. Rules, e.g.: (a) no names of departments, roles or software in the map — apply the test "would a reorganisation or a new system rename it?"; (b) name capabilities with a business object and an activity (noun + activity, e.g. *fleet management*), without times, places or systems; (c) one level per map: process steps belong to level 2 or to the process model, not to level 1.

Marking hint: half a point per box in part 1 (correct classification and sensible replacement); two points for a plausible tier assignment and one point per missing capability in part 2; one point per rule in part 3.
:::

## A Value Stream for Klinikverbund Oberrhein

*Klinikverbund Oberrhein* (sessions 3, 10 and 11) is a group of four hospitals that merged two years ago. Each hospital still has its own hospital information system and its own data centre. The board wants a **joint patient portal**. As a first step, the group CIO wants to understand the value stream **"receive a planned operation"** from the patient's point of view.

1. Name the **stakeholder** and the **value** of this value stream, and describe **five or six stages** from the first contact to the end of the follow-up.
2. Name the **enabling capabilities** for each stage (use level-1 capabilities such as *patient management*, *appointment and capacity management*, *medical treatment*, *care*, *billing*).
3. Which capability is **critical** for this value stream, and why? Which stages could the **patient portal** support?
4. Explain the difference between this value stream and the **business process** "admit a patient" in two sentences.

::: answer lines=14
:::

::: hint
A value stream is seen from the stakeholder's side and has only a few stages end to end. A capability that enables several stages is critical.
:::

::: solution
1. Stakeholder: **patient** (also accept the referring doctor as a secondary stakeholder). Value: **the operation is carried out successfully, safely and without unnecessary waiting**. Stages, e.g.: (1) referral and registration → (2) pre-operative consultation and planning → (3) admission → (4) operation and in-patient stay → (5) discharge → (6) follow-up and billing.
2. E.g. (1) patient management, appointment and capacity management; (2) medical treatment (diagnostics), appointment and capacity management, patient information and consent; (3) patient management, bed management; (4) medical treatment, care, medication management; (5) patient management, care (transition management), clinical documentation; (6) medical treatment (follow-up), billing (with health insurers).
3. **Patient management** (registration, admission, discharge — identity, master data and documents of the patient) and **appointment and capacity management** enable most stages; in a group with four separate hospital information systems both are likely to be fragmented, so they are the first candidates for investment. The portal can support stage 1 (online registration, upload of referral and findings), stage 2 (appointments, information and consent forms), stage 5 (discharge letter) and stage 6 (follow-up appointments, invoices). Accept also *clinical documentation* as critical with a reasoning.
4. The value stream shows the **end-to-end stages of value from the patient's view** and is stable and independent of the organisation; the business process "admit a patient" describes **in detail how one stage is carried out** — steps, roles, rules and systems — and can differ between the four hospitals.

Marking hint: two points for stakeholder, value and sensible stages; two points for the capability mapping; two points for part 3 (critical capability with reasoning, portal stages); two points for part 4.
:::

## Heat Map and Capability-Based Planning at Bodensee Reisen AG

*Bodensee Reisen AG* (sessions 2, 10 and 11), a tour operator with 25 travel agencies and a call centre, wants to sell personalised trips mainly online and through an app by 2030; the agencies will become "experience stores". The architecture team has rated selected capabilities and mapped the applications:

| Capability | Importance for the 2030 strategy | IT support today | Applications today |
|---|---|---|---|
| Customer management | High | Weak | Booking system (2005), newsletter tool, Excel lists in agencies |
| Trip design and personalisation | High | Missing | — |
| Booking and reservation | High | Partial | Booking system (2005), only usable by travel agents |
| Payment management | Medium | Good | External payment service provider |
| Supplier and contract management (hotels, buses) | Medium | Partial | Booking system, two spreadsheets |
| Financial management | Low | Good | Accounting package |
| Human resources management | Low | Partial | Payroll service, paper personnel files |

1. Draw the capabilities into a simple **2 × 2 matrix** (importance × IT support) and name the **three capabilities** that should receive investment first. Justify.
2. Find one **redundancy** and one **gap** in the application mapping.
3. The head of sales says: "We just need to buy a modern CRM." Explain, with the idea of **capability-based planning**, why the architecture team wants to discuss investments per capability instead. Formulate **two initiatives** for one of your three capabilities that cover more than software.
4. Human resources management is rated "partial". Why is it **not** a priority, and what could the company do instead?

::: answer lines=16
:::

::: hint
Combine two lenses: high importance and weak support means "invest". Low importance means "standardise, keep simple or buy as a service".
:::

::: solution
1. Priority (high importance, weak or missing support): **trip design and personalisation** (missing — a new capability the strategy depends on), **customer management** (weak, data spread over three places — personalisation needs one customer view), **booking and reservation** (partial — customers cannot book online themselves with the 2005 system). Payment management is medium and already good; financial management and HR are low importance.
2. Redundancy: customer data in the **booking system, the newsletter tool and Excel lists** (three places for one business object; also accept supplier data in booking system and two spreadsheets). Gap: **no application** for trip design and personalisation; also accept the gap "no self-service booking for customers".
3. Capability-based planning starts from the strategy and asks which **capabilities** must improve and how much; a CRM is only one possible means for one capability. A CRM alone would not fix the duplicate customer data in the booking system, the processes in the agencies, the consent for personalised marketing or the skills to use the data. Investments per capability make them comparable, show dependencies (personalisation needs customer management first) and justify IT spending with a business outcome. Initiatives for customer management, e.g.: define a **leading system** for customer data and migrate the newsletter and Excel lists (data); new **process** for collecting consent in agencies and online (process, GDPR); train agency staff to use the customer view (people); appoint a **business owner** for customer data (organisation). Accept similar sets for the other capabilities.
4. Low importance for the strategy: it does not differentiate the company. It should be supported **efficiently**, not innovatively — e.g. a standard HR software as a service, digitising personnel files as part of a standard solution, or outsourcing payroll completely (session 15). Money should go to the differentiating core capabilities first.

Marking hint: two points for part 1 (matrix and three justified priorities); two points for part 2; four points for part 3 (two for the argument, one per initiative); two points for part 4.
:::

## Exam-Style Task: Business Transformation at Kurpfalz Sanitär AG

*Kurpfalz Sanitär AG* (sessions 2 and 5–11), a wholesaler of bathroom fittings with 850 employees and €320 million turnover, sells about 70 % of its goods via its **online order platform** to 4,000 installers; orders received by 14:00 are delivered the same day from the central warehouse. The platform is hosted by an external provider and extended by six in-house developers; orders are passed to a 20-year-old, heavily customised **ERP system**. Sixty field sales representatives use tablets with an ordering app.

After the planned acquisition of a smaller **wholesaler in the Saarland** (own ERP system and web shop), the board wants to change the business model: the platform is to become an **open marketplace** on which manufacturers and other regional wholesalers also sell to installers, and installers get an **app for planning bathroom projects** with their end customers — including 3D planning, product lists and appointment booking. Kurpfalz Sanitär would earn commissions and a monthly fee for the planning app.

**Tasks:**

1. Explain what a **business model** is and name **three building blocks** of the Business Model Canvas that change with the marketplace and the planning app, with one concrete change each. (4 points)
2. Distinguish **digitalisation** from **digital transformation** and give one example of each from the case. (3 points)
3. Define a **business capability** and distinguish it from a **business process** and an **application**, using one example from the case for each. (3 points)
4. Sketch a **level-1 capability map** for Kurpfalz Sanitär AG after the acquisition with at least **ten** capabilities in **strategic, core and supporting** tiers. Mark **two new** capabilities that the marketplace requires. (5 points)
5. Explain how the architecture team can use the capability map with **two different heat map lenses** for the integration of the Saarland wholesaler and for the new business model. (3 points)
6. Which perspective of the **strategic alignment model** fits the planned change? Explain in two sentences how EAM helps to keep business and IT aligned. (2 points)

::: answer lines=30
:::

::: solution
Marking guide (20 points):

**1. Business model and building blocks (4 points)**

- Definition (1 point): a business model describes how an enterprise **creates** value for its customers, **delivers** it and **captures** a share of it as revenue and profit (Teece, 2010).
- Three building blocks with a concrete change (3 points, 1 each), e.g.: **key partnerships** — manufacturers and other wholesalers as sellers on the marketplace; **revenue streams** — commissions and monthly fees in addition to the trade margin; **value propositions** — one place to buy from many suppliers; project planning with end customers for installers; **customer segments** — installers plus manufacturers and wholesalers as platform customers, indirectly end customers; **key activities** — operating a marketplace, onboarding sellers, developing the app; **key resources** — platform, data on installers, end-customer projects; **channels** — app; **customer relationships** — from ordering to a daily working tool; **cost structure** — software development and platform operations.

**2. Digitalisation vs. digital transformation (3 points)**

- Distinction (1 point): digitalisation uses digital technology to improve existing processes; digital transformation triggers significant changes to the organisation, typically to the value proposition and business model.
- Example digitalisation (1 point): e.g. the tablet ordering app of field sales, the same-day order process via the platform, automated order transfer to the ERP.
- Example digital transformation (1 point): open marketplace with commissions, or the planning app with a monthly fee.

**3. Capability, process, application (3 points, 1 each)**

- Capability: what the enterprise must be able to do, stable and independent of organisation and systems — e.g. *order management*, *warehouse and delivery management*, *seller (partner) management*.
- Process: how it is done, in steps with roles and rules — e.g. "accept an order by 14:00, pick, pack and deliver the same day".
- Application: software that supports processes and thereby capabilities — e.g. the online order platform, the ERP system, the tablet ordering app.

**4. Capability map (5 points)**

- At least ten plausible capabilities named with the what/how test, no departments or systems (2 points), e.g.: strategic — strategy and business development, partner and supplier strategy, risk and compliance management; core — customer (installer) management, product and catalogue management, order management, warehouse and delivery management, field sales management, customer service, pricing and payment management; supporting — financial management, procurement, HR management, IT management.
- Sensible assignment to the three tiers (1 point).
- Two new capabilities for the marketplace (2 points, 1 each), e.g.: **seller (partner) onboarding and management**, **commission and settlement management**, **marketplace operation / quality management of offers**, **project planning for installers**, **end-customer appointment management**.

**5. Heat maps (3 points)**

- Integration of the Saarland wholesaler (1.5 points): e.g. lens **number of applications / redundancy** or **IT support quality per company** — capabilities supported twice (two ERPs, two web shops) are consolidation candidates; capabilities where the acquired company is stronger may keep its solution.
- New business model (1.5 points): e.g. lens **strategic importance** combined with **maturity or IT support** — new or weak capabilities with high importance (seller management, commission settlement, planning app) are investment priorities; this supports capability-based planning and the roadmap.
- Accept other lenses (cost, risk) if the use is explained.

**6. Strategic alignment (2 points)**

- Perspective (1 point): **competitive potential** — digital technology (platform, app) enables a new business strategy and business model; accept **technology transformation** if the student argues that the business strategy (marketplace) requires a new IT strategy and infrastructure.
- EAM (1 point): EAM provides a shared picture of capabilities, applications and data for both business and IT, shows the gaps and dependencies of the new business model (e.g. the 20-year-old ERP, two ERPs after the acquisition), and turns the strategy into a target architecture and roadmap that projects are reviewed against.
:::
